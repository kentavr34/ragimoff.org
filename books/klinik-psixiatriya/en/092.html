1. Definition and nosology

Prominent personality traits or patterns (ICD-11: 6D11 Prominent personality traits or patterns) is not a stand-alone diagnosis but a set of qualifiers: they record which trait domains a personality disorder (6D10) or personality difficulty is expressed through. Severity is coded in 6D10 and the domains here; 6D11 is never assigned on its own.

Personality difficulty is not a mental disorder in ICD-11 — it sits in the chapter “Factors influencing health status” under the code QE50.7. Personality traits cause functional difficulty but fall short of the 6D10 criteria.

2. History

  1. ICD-11 (2019) — the 10 categorical personality disorders were dropped in favor of a severity rating (6D10) plus five trait domains (6D11.0–6D11.4).
  2. The borderline pattern (6D11.5) was added separately as the successor to ICD-10 borderline personality disorder (F60.3); in DSM-5-TR the corresponding diagnosis is Section II borderline PD (F60.3).

3. Epidemiology

Precise epidemiological data are limited; subclinical personality traits are widespread in the population.

4. Etiology and pathogenesis

Same as 6D10 — genetic and environmental interaction.

5. Clinical features

  • 6D11.0 Negative affectivity — a broad range of negative emotions: anxiousness, irritability, low self-esteem, emotional lability.
  • 6D11.1 Detachment — social and emotional distance: avoidance, restricted affect, refusal of intimacy.
  • 6D11.2 Dissociality — disregard for the rights and feelings of others: self-centeredness, lack of empathy, exploitativeness.
  • 6D11.3 Disinhibition — impulsivity, distractibility, irresponsibility, a pull towards immediate reward.
  • 6D11.4 Anankastia — perfectionism, rigid rules, emotional and behavioral control.
  • 6D11.5 Borderline pattern — instability of relationships, self-image and affect, impulsivity, fear of abandonment, self-harm and suicidal behavior.

6. Diagnosis

6.1 Unified diagnostic criteria

A. The diagnosis is made under 6D10 (personality disorder) or QE50.7 (personality difficulty); 6D11 is added only as a qualifier.

B. All domains present to a clinically significant degree are recorded — there may be several.

C. The borderline pattern (6D11.5) may be recorded in addition to any combination of domains.

6.2 Differences between sources

ICD-11 — category somewhat similar to the Z-code for ‘clinical attention to personality problems.’

6.3 Diagnostic algorithm

  • Clinical interview.
  • 5-domain assessment.
  • Exclusion of full PD diagnosis.

6.4 Differential diagnosis

Condition Distinguishing feature
Personality Disorder (6D10) Significant functional impairment.
Adjustment disorder (6B43) Response to stress factor.
Normal personality variant No functional difficulty.

7. Examination and assessment

Personality inventories (PID-5).

8. Treatment

  1. Psychotherapy — short-to-medium term; problem-focused CBT or supportive.
  2. Stress management and coping skills.
  3. Specific intervention for comorbid symptoms.

8.1 Treatment methods

  1. DSM-5 — Personality Inventory for DSM-5 (PID-5) — Dimensional personality scale.

9. Prognosis

Improvement with treatment; risk of progression to persistent or full PD.

10. Myths and misconceptions

Myth 1: “Personality difficulty is not a real diagnosis”

Evidence: ICD-11 is the official code; it makes clinical attention to subclinical personality problems possible.

Myth 2: “Personality difficulty inevitably turns into PD”

Evidence: Some patients experience deterioration, but most stabilize or improve.

Myth 3: “Should only be treated with medication”

Evidence: Psychotherapy is first-line; pharmacotherapy for comorbid symptoms.

Myth 4: “This category is only for labeling ‘anxious’ patients”

Evidence: ICD-11 — clinical response to functional difficulty; provides clinical support.
