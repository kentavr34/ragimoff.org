SECTION 1. THE GAME PRACTICE “THE PHOENIX ERA” · The Concept of the Game

The game practice The Phoenix Era is a living organism within the methodology. It is a space where theory becomes experience and words turn into feelings. The game was not created for winning in the classic sense — it was created for awakening. It is a mirror in which each participant sees not who they want to appear to be, but who they truly are.

The mission of the game: by living through the twelve relationship models, the participant recognizes their neurotic patterns, sees them from the outside, and receives the key to moving into a healthy model of behavior — the Phoenix model.

Format: group dynamics with 8 to 24 people. One full cycle of the game takes about 2–3 hours. Participants: Players, the Author, the Phoenix, the Master.

Game Technique: Three Circles of Rebirth

The game is built on the principle of ascent. It consists of three rounds, each of which opens a new level of awareness.

Round One: The Labyrinth of Masks (45 minutes)

Goal: to reveal hidden roles and find the Phoenix. At the start of the game, each participant is dealt a face-down deck of cards. Each card shows one of the 12 relationship models. The player sees their own card but must keep it hidden from the others. This is their secret role, their “mask” for this game.

1. The Situation: The Author describes a life situation — a family conflict, a workplace dilemma, an ethical choice, or a crisis. Example: “You’ve found a wallet with a large sum of money inside. No one is around. What do you do?”

2. The Reaction: The Players enter into dialogue. Each speaks and acts strictly according to their hidden card. Lilith will suggest keeping the money. Teresa will suggest donating it to charity. The Beggar will insist to everyone that the find must be taken. The Phoenix will suggest finding the owner.

3. The Hunt: The Author observes the behavior, analyzing words, intonations, logic. Their goal is to figure out who is who.

4. The Verdict: The Author points to a player and names their model. If the Author is right, the player reveals their card and is out of the game. If the Author is wrong, they are out themselves, the decks are reshuffled, and a new Author is appointed.

Round One ends only when the Phoenix has been found. This is the key condition. As long as the Phoenix stays hidden, the masks cannot be fully cast off.

Round Two: The Light of the Phoenix (45–60 minutes)

Goal: to see the healthy model in action. The Author: the player who held the Phoenix card and was found in Round One. Now they become the guide.

1. The Request: The remaining Players bring forward difficult, tangled situations from their real lives — unresolved conflicts, fears, dead ends.

2. The Phoenix’s Answer: The Phoenix gives no ready-made advice. They show what they would do in the player’s place, demonstrating Sincerity, Love, and Mercy in action.

3. The Dialogue: The Players may argue with the Phoenix. The Phoenix’s task is to hold the light without sliding into neurotic reactions (resentment, aggression, excuses).

Round Three: The Path of the Master (45–60 minutes)

Goal: therapeutic integration and consolidation of the experience. The Author: the Master (a professional therapist, a trainer of the Phoenix School who has completed special certification).

1. The Analysis: the Master reviews the game that has just been played, showing the Players where they acted out of trauma and where they acted out of awareness.

2. The Work: the Players receive assignments — to relive a situation from within the Phoenix model. The task sounds like this: “Try saying no not with Puttana’s aggression, but with the Phoenix’s confidence.”

3. The Therapy: the Master helps the participants connect their in-game reactions to their real-life scripts.

Closing: a discussion circle. Each participant formulates one intention: which model they choose in real life after the game.
