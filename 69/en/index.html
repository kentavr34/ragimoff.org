The myths that keep people from starting treatment

Every chapter of the textbook has a section ‘Myths and False Beliefs’ — 104 chapters, around a thousand breakdowns. Here are four of them: the ones that most often stand between a person and help.

Depression

“Depression is simply sadness, it can be removed by an effort of will”

Depression is a condition with mechanisms, a course and a treatment. Willpower does not work not because the person is weak, but because that is how the illness is built. Antidepressants do not cause dependence and do not change personality — this is one of the seven myths in the chapter.

Clinical Psychiatry · chapter on depressive disorders · 11 myths with breakdowns

The full list of myths about depression →
Anxiety disorders

“Anxiety is an ordinary feeling, not a medical condition”

An anxiety disorder is a diagnosis with criteria, not a character trait. And it is not treated by “trying not to be anxious”: a person cannot learn not to be anxious by an act of will, any more than they can order themselves not to be ill.

Clinical Psychiatry · chapter on anxiety disorders · 10 myths with breakdowns

The full list of myths about anxiety →
Obsessive-compulsive disorder

“OCD is simply a character trait linked to cleanliness”

OCD is a disorder in which a person loses control over their own actions, and giving yourself permission to perform compulsions does not soothe but reinforces the symptom. “Willpower” here is not a tool but part of the trap.

Clinical Psychiatry · chapter on OCD · 7 myths with breakdowns

The full list of myths about OCD →
Nocturnal enuresis

“Punishment or reproach will make the child stop”

Enuresis is not stubbornness or laziness, and punishment does not treat it but adds shame, which stops parents from seeking help in time. This is one of the six mistakes examined in the chapter.
