Myths and facts

What they say — and what is true

Every explanation cites its source. Check it yourself: open the textbook and read the section named.

Depression MYTH

“Depression is just a bad mood — it will pass”

Fact: In fact: depression is a diagnosable illness — brain chemistry, sleep, appetite and capacity to work all change. It lasts six to eight months on average and recurs without treatment. Psychotherapy and medication are equally evidenced methods.

Source: “Clinical Psychiatry”, the depression section Open the book →

Social phobia MYTH

“It is just shyness — pull yourself together”

Fact: In fact: social phobia is not shyness. The person avoids speaking, eating, making calls; physical reactions set in and life narrows. The effect of CBT is measurable: symptoms fall and become manageable.

Source: “Clinical Psychiatry” + “The Virus of Guilt” See “The Virus of Guilt” →

Nocturnal enuresis MYTH

“The child is lazy and does it on purpose”

Fact: In fact: nocturnal enuresis is not a matter of the child’s will — maturation, heredity and sleep phases are involved. Punishment makes it worse. The evidenced path: routine, alarm device, behavioural therapy.

Source: “Clinical Psychiatry”, the enuresis section Open the book →

Panic attack MYTH

“A panic attack is a heart attack — I am dying”

Fact: In fact: a panic attack is not dangerous and does not cause death. Racing heart, breathlessness and fear of dying are a wave of adrenaline that subsides within ten to twenty minutes. The real damage is avoidance and anticipatory fear — and that is what therapy removes.

Source: “Clinical Psychiatry”, anxiety disorders Open the book →

Psychotherapy MYTH

“Talking does not cure illness”

Fact: In fact: the effectiveness of psychotherapy has been measured in hundreds of randomised trials and is comparable to medication. The condition is working to protocol and measuring the result.

Source: international protocols and consensus See the programmes →
