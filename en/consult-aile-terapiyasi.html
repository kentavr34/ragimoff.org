FAMILY THERAPY

Family therapy

A systemic approach for couples and families — communication, conflict resolution and restoring the emotional bond.

Book a visit

Who needs this therapy?

A crisis in the relationship and loss of trust, a recurring cycle of conflict.

  1. Constant conflicts

    Repeated arguments on the same topics, with no resolution, wear down the family system and push the partners apart.

  2. Lack of communication

    The partners do not hear each other, cannot express their feelings, and live in silent tension.

  3. Crisis of trust

    Infidelity, hidden truths or wounds from the past have struck a blow to the relationship.

  4. On the brink of divorce

    The partners are thinking about separating, but want to know whether the family can be preserved.

  5. Parent-child tension

    There is disagreement between the partners about the relationship with the child and about discipline.

  6. Sexual incompatibility

    Problems in the sphere of intimacy are not discussed, which deepens the emotional distance.

How is the therapy structured?

Joint intake → individual work → together again. Four stages leading from crisis to calm.

  1. 01

    Joint initial intake

    The couple is received by both specialists together. The real theme of the conflict — often not the one being argued about — is brought to the surface and named openly.

  2. 02

    Individual stage

    The partners separate: Kenan Ragimov works with the man, Samira Ragimova with the woman. Each explores their own part not in front of the partner, but in safe conditions.

  3. 03

    Joint session again

    The results of the individual work are brought together. The couple now speaks not in the language of accusation, but with a new tool of communication — this is practised in the session.

  4. 04

    Consolidation

    Positive changes are stabilised. The goal is not a family without conflict, but a family able to resolve conflict on its own.

This format is built for couples on the brink of divorce, with persistent conflict and unbearable emotional tension. The path begins in a state of crisis and ends with calm and harmony in the relationship.

Frequently asked questions

The questions asked most often about therapy and training — with precise answers.

Can I come alone, or must we come as a couple?

You can come alone. The work often begins with one partner reaching out. In the systemic approach, a change in one participant’s behaviour sets the whole family system in motion. The partner can join later — this speeds up the process, but it is not a condition.

How many sessions are needed?

The number of stages is determined after the joint initial intake. In crisis situations the first result is usually visible after 4–6 sessions; restoring a relationship requires a longer process. The exact number is given at the initial intake.

Is the online format as effective as in person?

In couple therapy the online format follows the same protocol. The only difference is the price — an online session is cheaper.

Does what we say stay confidential?

Yes. What is said at the individual stage is not automatically disclosed to the partner — what is brought to the joint session is agreed with you. This is the main condition of the individual stage.

Is a psychology education required to join the training?

A higher education is not required. The programme is intended for psychologists, school psychologists, teachers, coaches, students and parents. For those with a psychology education the opportunities are wider: graduates can offer counselling services at our centre.

What document is issued at the end of the training?

At the end of the course an exam is held and a certificate of the International Psychotherapy Association (IPAS) is issued.
